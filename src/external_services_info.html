Networking troubleshooting

Togetherness Table keeps your table on your own device. To play with other people it needs a few small helper services to introduce your devices to each other. This page explains what each one does, when you need it, and what to do when its light in the Peers tab isn’t green.

What the lights mean

Working. Partly working, or running on a backup. Not working. Hover a light in the Peers tab for the reason. Not needed, not tested yet (no one has connected), or paused by offline mode.

Which services do I need?

*Some environments, like offices, airports, and cell connections, block the direct paths between devices that Togetherness normally uses.

Working alone needs none of this. Your table is saved on your device and every light can be red without losing a thing. Changes you make offline sync the next time you and your peers connect.

Signalling

The introduction desk.

What it’s for

When you open a shared table, your device needs to find the others who have it open. A signalling server passes along the “hello, here’s how to reach me” messages. It never sees your table: once two devices have been introduced, they talk to each other directly.

Do I need it?

To meet new peers, yes. Peers who are already connected stay connected if it goes down. Not needed for solo or offline editing.

If the light isn’t green

  • Check your internet connection.
  • Togetherness tries two servers at once. One red and one green is fine.
  • Both down? Try again in a few minutes, or point at a different server in Advanced.

STUN

“What does the outside world call me?”

What it’s for

Most devices sit behind a home router and don’t know their own public address. A STUN server tells your device what address the internet sees, so a friend’s device can reach it directly. It’s a quick question and answer, with no table data and almost no bandwidth.

Do I need it?

For playing with peers, yes. Togetherness includes sensible public defaults, and running your own is easy.

If the light isn’t green

  • Grey means no one has connected yet, so nothing has been tested.
  • Red means STUN answers never arrived. A firewall may be blocking them, or the server may be down.
  • You can set your own in Advanced.

TURN Creds

The key to the relay.

What it’s for

A TURN relay (next section) isn’t free to run, so it can’t be open to everyone. The TURN Credentials Service hands your device a short-lived username and password for the relay, so the permanent key is never stored in the app. It is the default way Togetherness gets a relay.

Do I need it?

Only when your connection needs a relay. If you enter your own TURN server in Advanced, this light is grey because it isn’t used.

If the light isn’t green

  • Amber: the service couldn’t be reached, perhaps because you’re offline. Togetherness falls back to a public test relay, which is fine for trying things out but not something to rely on.
  • Red: the service has used up its monthly allowance. It stops handing out relay access so the bill can’t run away. Peers who can connect directly are unaffected. Peers on strict networks may not connect until the allowance resets next month, or until you use your own relay.

TURN

The go-between of last resort.

What it’s for

Some networks, like offices, airports, and cell connections, don’t let devices connect to each other directly. A TURN relay sits in the middle and passes traffic back and forth. Your table stays encrypted between you and your peers, and the relay only forwards scrambled data it can’t read.

Do I need it?

Often not. On most home networks, peers connect directly and the relay is never used. When a peer is on a strict network, it can be the only way through.

If the light isn’t green

  • Grey means no one has connected yet.
  • Red means the relay never answered or none is configured. Check TURN Creds first.
  • If you see “public test relay”, you’re on a shared free relay. It works for a try-out but can disappear.

You own this software

Togetherness Table belongs to the people who use it. None of these services is a gate you have to pass through. Every one can be swapped for your own in Advanced, and you’re free to run them yourself or pay a company to run them for you.
